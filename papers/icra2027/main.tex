\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide open-vocabulary manipulation policies, but their inference rates are incompatible with the 100\,Hz joint control loops required by humanoid robots such as the Unitree G1 (29\,DoF).
We motivate a hybrid controller that keeps the VLA as a sparse language-conditioned intent generator and inserts a fixed Echo State Network (ESN) reservoir to bridge held joint targets to smooth 100\,Hz commands; only a linear readout is trained by ridge regression.
On a DGX Station (Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB), live \texttt{UnifoLM-VLA-Base} profiling on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table} yields a $\sim$57$\times$ gap to 100\,Hz (PyTorch mean \textbf{570.7\,ms} / \textbf{1.752\,Hz}; Nsight NVTX \textbf{593.7\,ms} / \textbf{1.684\,Hz}).
ESN training and evaluation use \texttt{G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} (200 episodes, $\sim$0.70\,h; train $0$--$159$ / held-out $160$--$199$).
A multi-episode CUDA ESN ($\alpha{=}0.3$, $\lambda{=}1$) reaches held-out open-loop RMSE \textbf{$2.78\times10^{-3}\!\pm\!3.44\times10^{-3}$\,rad} ($\sim$5.6$\times$ better than linear upsampling) and MuJoCo \emph{dataset-oracle} RMSE \textbf{$3.00\times10^{-3}\!\pm\!3.56\times10^{-3}$\,rad} on all 40 held-out episodes, with oracle grasp / task success \textbf{100\%} and mean table-contact ratio \textbf{74.5\%}.
Live UnifoLM dual-process control clears 100\,Hz on average for all bridges; ESN mean step is 6.92\,ms (145\,Hz) with GPU-contention tails above 10\,ms.
Under the same live UnifoLM stack with interactive cloth, a proximity-synthetic Dex1 proxy, table-plane press prior, and motion clamps (frozen stance legs, EE workspace / joint limits, crawl rate-limit) yields \textbf{100\%} table contact and \textbf{task success} for ESN over 30\,s without the prior flailing failure mode.
S2R/DIRT deploy is the next step.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Motivation: Why VLAs Break on Humanoids}
Foundation VLAs such as OpenVLA~\cite{OpenVLA2025}, $\pi_0$~\cite{Preprints2026}, and Unitree UnifoLM-VLA~\cite{UnifoLM2026} map language and vision to robot actions and have unlocked open-vocabulary manipulation on fixed-base arms.
Humanoid whole-body control, however, is a different regime~\cite{PhysicalAI2026}: the Unitree G1 exposes a \textbf{29-DoF} joint interface that must be served near \textbf{100\,Hz} (10\,ms) for balance-preserving tracking, contact-rich wiping, and safe impedance behavior.
Autoregressive and diffusion action heads spend hundreds of milliseconds per inference.
Under a naive zero-order hold (ZOH), each sparse VLA target is frozen for tens of control ticks; the resulting staircase joint trajectories inject high jerk, excite unmodeled dynamics, and fail contact tasks even when the semantic plan is correct.
We call this mismatch the \emph{frequency gap}: the VLA may be ``right'' about \emph{what} to do, yet too slow for \emph{how} the humanoid must move.

Two further constraints sharpen the problem.
First, simply training a larger VLA to run faster is rarely feasible on edge or lab GPUs already saturated by vision--language backbones; the control layer must therefore be \emph{cheap} and preferably trained without backpropagation through time.
Second, classical upsamplers (ZOH, linear interpolation, joint PID toward held setpoints) ignore the robot's proprioceptive state between VLA updates and cannot learn demonstration-consistent dynamics.
This motivates a hybrid stack: keep the VLA as a sparse, language-conditioned intent generator, and insert a fast dynamical bridge that turns held targets into smooth 100\,Hz commands conditioned on current joints.

\subsection{Approach and Scope}
We treat the G1 as a unified dynamical system and insert a leaky Echo State Network (ESN) between measured VLA intents ($\sim$1.7\,Hz on UnifoLM-VLA-Base) and joint commands (100\,Hz).
The VLA supplies sparse targets $q^{\star}_{\mathrm{VLA}}$; the reservoir supplies reflexive temporal filtering with a ridge-trained linear readout and \emph{fixed} recurrent weights.
Training and evaluation use Unitree's public \texttt{G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} demonstrations (Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}): a single-task wipe corpus whose scale and episode-level split we declare explicitly so that reported RMSE and grasp metrics are tied to a known data distribution---not to an unspecified ``demo.''

\textbf{Contributions.}
(i)~Dual-instrument UnifoLM-VLA-Base latency on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table}: 100-trial PyTorch profiler + Nsight Systems NVTX/CUDA timeline, quantifying a $\sim$57--59$\times$ gap to 100\,Hz;
(ii)~CUDA ESN with $u(t)=[q_t;\,q^{\star}_{\mathrm{VLA}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$ and ridge readout, with a measured leak$\times$ridge sweep on wipe-table demonstrations and a declared train/held-out episode split;
(iii)~GIL-free dual-process MuJoCo$+$bridge control comparing ESN against ZOH, linear, and PID under live UnifoLM (timing);
(iv)~MuJoCo wipe-table \emph{dataset-oracle} evaluation on all 40 held-out episodes (joint RMSE, grasp, contact, coverage), separated from live closed-loop success;
(v)~Live UnifoLM wipe-success trials with interactive cloth (proximity-synthetic Dex1 proxy) reporting grasp/contact/task metrics under ESN vs.\ ZOH;
(vi)~S2R ZMQ deploy package (29-DoF defaults) for DIRT-guided transfer.

\section{Related Work}
VLA surveys highlight bimanual and whole-body settings as hard cases~\cite{VLA_Review2026}.
Flow matching accelerates action generation~\cite{Preprints2026}; recurrent bridges such as xLSTM help long-horizon memory~\cite{Notes2Self2026}.
Diffusion action chunking trades compute for smoothness; Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) provide rhythmic priors but lack language grounding.
Reservoir computing offers fast, training-light dynamics~\cite{Jaeger2001,Caoetal2023}.
We combine a frozen reservoir with a language-conditioned VLA rather than replacing either.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perception / VLA:} \texttt{UnifoLM-VLA-Base} (backbone \texttt{UnifoLM-VLM-Base}) emits 29-D joint actions for clean/wipe-table goals (e.g., \texttt{g1\_clean\_table}); EE-space variants map poses to joints via Jacobian IK when needed.
  \item \textbf{ESN bridge:} Leaky reservoir update
  \[
  x(t)=(1-\alpha)x(t-1)+\alpha\,\tanh\!\big(W_{\mathrm{res}}x(t-1)+W_{\mathrm{in}}u(t)\big),
  \]
  with sparse $W_{\mathrm{res}}$ ($N{=}1000$, $\rho{=}0.95$, sparsity $0.9$) and readout
  $y(t)=W_{\mathrm{out}}[x(t);u(t)]$ fit by ridge regression on 100\,Hz joints with VLA tokens held for 50 ticks ($u\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^{29}$).
  We sweep $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.1,0.3\}$ and $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2},1\}$ and select by RMSE$\times$jerk score.
  \item \textbf{S2R / DIRT layer:} Parameterizes sim--real discrepancy for controller and domain ablations (ESN vs.\ passthrough ZOH/raw).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pure VLA ZOH:} hold each $\sim$1.7\,Hz target for $\sim$57--59 ticks at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ linear interpolation:} interpolate between sparse knots at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ PID:} classical joint feedback toward the held VLA target (does not close the rate gap by itself).
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ ESN (ours):} CUDA reservoir + ridge readout.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Dataset Distribution and Claim Scope}
\label{sec:dataset}
All ESN training, offline upsample baselines, and MuJoCo wipe-oracle evaluations use
\texttt{unitreerobotics/G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} (Hugging Face \texttt{train} split).
Table~\ref{tab:dataset} summarizes the corpus.
There are \textbf{200} demonstration episodes ($75{,}909$ frames at $\sim$30\,Hz capture; $\sim$0.70\,h total),
each lasting roughly $9.9$--$16.8$\,s (mean $12.6$\,s).
Observations include 29-D whole-body joints (\texttt{observation.body}), bimanual arms/grippers, and EE poses;
\texttt{task\_index} is identically $0$ (wipe only)---there is no free-text language field in this release.
We therefore restrict scientific claims to \emph{this} wipe-table distribution on the G1 Dex1 embodiment, not to general multi-task humanoid skill.

\textbf{Episode-level split.}
To avoid temporal leakage from random frame shuffles, we hold out the last 20\% of episodes by index:
\textbf{train} episodes $0$--$159$ ($160$ eps, $60{,}763$ frames) and
\textbf{held-out} episodes $160$--$199$ ($40$ eps, $15{,}146$ frames).
Hyperparameter sweeps may use a train subset for speed; the deployed readout should be fit on the train pool and scored on held-out episodes.
Reporting train-episode success as ``generalization'' is invalid under this protocol.
Dataset-oracle MuJoCo replay (proprio $+$ held demo tokens) is likewise \emph{not} live UnifoLM closed-loop evidence.

\subsection{Setup}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robot / hardware:} Unitree G1 (29\,DoF); NVIDIA DGX Station, Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB.
  \item \textbf{Profiling:} Live UnifoLM-VLA-Base on G1 Clean-Table (FP16,
    \texttt{unnorm\_key}=\texttt{g1\_clean\_table}, 64 new tokens).
    PyTorch wall-clock ($n{=}100$) and Nsight Systems NVTX on \texttt{infer()}.
  \item \textbf{ESN training:} CUDA ridge on all 160 train episodes
    (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}; 100\,Hz joints; 2\,Hz held VLA tokens; washout 50).
    Hyperparameters $(\alpha,\lambda)=(0.3,1)$ from the ep.~0 leak$\times$ridge sweep
    (Table~\ref{tab:esn_sweep}); readout scored on held-out episodes $160$--$199$.
  \item \textbf{Dual-process (timing):} Live UnifoLM $\leftrightarrow$ MuJoCo$+$bridge
    (\texttt{esn}/\texttt{zoh}/\texttt{linear}/\texttt{pid}) @ 100\,Hz target.
  \item \textbf{Wipe oracle:} Dataset-oracle proprio + held demo tokens through the trained ESN
    on all held-out episodes (kinematic, mocap cloth); not live closed-loop VLA success.
  \item \textbf{Live wipe success:} Same interactive cloth + wipe metrics under live UnifoLM
    (ESN vs.\ ZOH, 30\,s); Dex1 width via proximity-synthetic proxy (UnifoLM EE actions have no gripper channel).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Remaining Closed-Loop Study}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\paragraph{Frequency gap.}
Table~\ref{tab:measured} cross-validates the gap with two instruments.
The latest PyTorch campaign reports \textbf{570.7\,ms / 1.752\,Hz} (std\,10.1\,ms, 0\% parse failure): naive ZOH must hold each VLA target for roughly 57 control ticks at 100\,Hz.
Nsight NVTX reports \textbf{593.7\,ms} mean ($\approx$1.68\,Hz; $59.4\times$ gap); the $\sim$20\,ms uplift vs.\ wall-clock is consistent with tracing overhead.
Both profilers attribute GPU time to large GEMMs; Nsight additionally highlights H2D pressure (\texttt{cudaMemcpyAsync}), motivating pinned transfers and keeping the ESN on-device between sparse VLA updates.

\paragraph{Data scope.}
Table~\ref{tab:dataset} bounds where our model is allowed to ``work'': one wipe task, $\sim$0.7\,h of Dex1 demos, episode-level 80/20 split.
Gains on this corpus do not imply multi-task humanoid competence.
Held-out open-loop and oracle tables in Table~\ref{tab:measured} replace earlier single-episode development numbers for generalization claims.

\paragraph{ESN and control timing.}
\mbox{Table~\ref{tab:esn_sweep}} shows that ridge strength dominates tracking on wipe ep.~0: $\lambda{=}1$ outperforms weaker regularization, and $(\alpha,\lambda)=(0.3,1)$ reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} RMSE with jerk nearly matched to the demonstration (ratio 0.981).
Under-regularized settings inflate RMSE by up to $\sim$60$\times$, so a short ridge sweep is necessary.
Retraining the readout on all 160 train episodes yields held-out open-loop RMSE \textbf{$2.78\times10^{-3}$\,rad}---about \textbf{5.6$\times$} better than linear upsampling (Table~\ref{tab:offline})---while train-ref ep.~0 RMSE remains $\sim$$1.1\times10^{-3}$\,rad.
Training remains cheap ($\sim$2.75\,kHz on V100), enabling $N$/$\rho$ ablations.
Under \emph{live} UnifoLM, dual-process ESN mean step is 6.92\,ms (145\,Hz) with steady max 12.6\,ms: the mean clears 100\,Hz, but GPU contention with the VLA produces tails we report rather than hide.
Classical bridges (ZOH/linear/PID) stay under 8\,ms steady max in the same live setting.
The MuJoCo wipe \emph{oracle} on all 40 held-out episodes shows tight joint tracking ($3.00\times10^{-3}$\,rad) with 100\% grasp and 74.5\% mean table contact when demo tokens drive the ESN---so the bridge+scene can wipe when the plan is correct.
Matching \emph{live} UnifoLM wipe trials (30\,s, interactive cloth) attach under a proximity-synthetic grasp; without a table-plane press prior the VLA lifts the cloth and contact collapses ($\sim$5\%).
Enabling \texttt{press\_table} (held cloth follows hand XY on the table surface) restores \textbf{100\%} contact and task success for ESN and ZOH---a geometric wipe prior, not learned press-from-vision.
Orientation IK currently tracks EE position only; UnifoLM EE actions also lack an explicit Dex1 gripper channel.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a VLA$+$ESN architecture targeting the \textbf{$\sim$57$\times$} gap between measured UnifoLM-VLA-Base inference (571\,ms / 1.752\,Hz PyTorch; 594\,ms / 1.684\,Hz Nsight) and G1 100\,Hz control.
A multi-episode CUDA ridge ESN reaches held-out open-loop RMSE \textbf{$2.78\times10^{-3}$\,rad} ($\sim$5.6$\times$ vs.\ linear) and MuJoCo dataset-oracle RMSE \textbf{$3.00\times10^{-3}$\,rad} on 40 held-out wipe episodes, with oracle grasp/task success \textbf{100\%} (mean contact 74.5\%).
Live UnifoLM dual-process control sustains \textbf{145\,Hz} mean ESN rate (steady tails above 10\,ms under GPU contention).
Live wipe trials with the same interactive cloth, proximity-synthetic grasp, and table-plane press prior reach \textbf{100\%} contact and task success for ESN and ZOH (30\,s)---motivating S2R/DIRT transfer without claiming vision-learned press toward ICRA 2027.
