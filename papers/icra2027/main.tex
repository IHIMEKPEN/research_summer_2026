\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide open-vocabulary manipulation policies, but their inference rates are incompatible with the 100\,Hz joint control loops required by humanoid robots such as the Unitree G1 (29\,DoF).
We propose a hybrid controller in which a fixed Echo State Network (ESN) reservoir bridges sparse VLA joint targets to smooth 100\,Hz commands.
Only a linear readout is trained by ridge regression; the reservoir weights remain fixed.
On a DGX Station (Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB), live \texttt{UnifoLM-VLA-Base} profiling on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table} yields a $\sim$57--59$\times$ gap to 100\,Hz: PyTorch mean latency \textbf{573.2\,ms} (\textbf{1.745\,Hz}; $n{=}100$), and Nsight Systems NVTX mean \textbf{593.7\,ms} (\textbf{1.684\,Hz}).
A CUDA ESN on \texttt{G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} ep.~0 reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} RMSE ($\alpha{=}0.3$, $\lambda{=}1$), a dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN loop sustains \textbf{248\,Hz} under mock VLA, and kinematic wipe-oracle replay achieves \textbf{$1.37\times10^{-3}$\,rad} joint RMSE with successful cloth grasp.
Live UnifoLM closed-loop baselines and an S2R/DIRT deploy path remain ongoing work.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Foundation VLAs such as OpenVLA~\cite{OpenVLA2025}, $\pi_0$~\cite{Preprints2026}, and Unitree UnifoLM-VLA~\cite{UnifoLM2026} map language and vision to robot actions.
Humanoid whole-body control, however, demands millisecond-scale updates~\cite{PhysicalAI2026}.
Autoregressive or diffusion action heads introduce latency that, under naive hold, yields jerky motion and task failure---the \emph{frequency gap}.

We treat the G1 as a unified dynamical system and insert a leaky ESN between VLA intents ($\sim$1.7\,Hz measured) and joint commands (100\,Hz).
The VLA supplies sparse targets; the reservoir supplies reflexive temporal filtering without backpropagation through time.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(i)~Dual-instrument UnifoLM-VLA-Base latency on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table}: 100-trial PyTorch profiler + Nsight Systems NVTX/CUDA timeline;
(ii)~CUDA ESN with $u(t)=[q_t;\,q^{\star}_{\mathrm{VLA}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$ and ridge readout, with a measured leak$\times$ridge sweep on wipe-table demonstrations;
(iii)~GIL-free dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN control with measured $>100$\,Hz headroom under mock VLA;
(iv)~MuJoCo wipe-table oracle evaluation of the trained ESN (joint RMSE, grasp, cloth path);
(v)~S2R ZMQ deploy package (29-DoF defaults) for DIRT-guided transfer.

\section{Related Work}
VLA surveys highlight bimanual and whole-body settings as hard cases~\cite{VLA_Review2026}.
Flow matching accelerates action generation~\cite{Preprints2026}; recurrent bridges such as xLSTM help long-horizon memory~\cite{Notes2Self2026}.
Diffusion action chunking trades compute for smoothness; Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) provide rhythmic priors but lack language grounding.
Reservoir computing offers fast, training-light dynamics~\cite{Jaeger2001,Caoetal2023}.
We combine a frozen reservoir with a language-conditioned VLA rather than replacing either.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perception / VLA:} \texttt{UnifoLM-VLA-Base} (backbone \texttt{UnifoLM-VLM-Base}) emits 29-D joint actions for clean/wipe-table goals (e.g., \texttt{g1\_clean\_table}); EE-space variants map poses to joints via Jacobian IK when needed.
  \item \textbf{ESN bridge:} Leaky reservoir update
  \[
  x(t)=(1-\alpha)x(t-1)+\alpha\,\tanh\!\big(W_{\mathrm{res}}x(t-1)+W_{\mathrm{in}}u(t)\big),
  \]
  with sparse $W_{\mathrm{res}}$ ($N{=}1000$, $\rho{=}0.95$, sparsity $0.9$) and readout
  $y(t)=W_{\mathrm{out}}[x(t);u(t)]$ fit by ridge regression on 100\,Hz joints with VLA tokens held for 50 ticks ($u\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^{29}$).
  We sweep $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.1,0.3\}$ and $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2},1\}$ and select by RMSE$\times$jerk score.
  \item \textbf{S2R / DIRT layer:} Parameterizes sim--real discrepancy for controller and domain ablations (ESN vs.\ passthrough ZOH/raw).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pure VLA ZOH:} hold each $\sim$1.7\,Hz target for $\sim$57--59 ticks at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ linear interpolation:} interpolate between sparse knots at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ ESN (ours):} CUDA reservoir + ridge readout.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Setup}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Robot / hardware:} Unitree G1 (29\,DoF); NVIDIA DGX Station, Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB.
  \item \textbf{Profiling:} Live UnifoLM-VLA-Base on G1 Clean-Table (FP16,
    \texttt{unnorm\_key}=\texttt{g1\_clean\_table}, 64 new tokens).
    PyTorch wall-clock ($n{=}100$) and Nsight Systems NVTX on \texttt{infer()}.
  \item \textbf{ESN training:} CUDA ridge on G1 Dex1 Wipe-Table ep.~0
    (1181 steps after washout 50; 100\,Hz control / 2\,Hz VLA hold).
  \item \textbf{Dual-process:} Mock VLA @ 2\,Hz $\leftrightarrow$ MuJoCo$+$CUDA ESN @ 100\,Hz (10\,s).
  \item \textbf{Wipe eval:} Dataset-oracle proprio + held VLA tokens through the trained ESN
    (kinematic, 6000 steps, mocap cloth).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics}
Task success (grasp / wipe contact), joint and right-EE RMSE, trajectory jerk ($d^3q/dt^3$), control Hz / step latency, GPU memory, action-parse failure rate, and CUDA operator shares.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Table~\ref{tab:measured} cross-validates the frequency gap with two instruments.
The PyTorch campaign establishes a stable \textbf{573\,ms / 1.745\,Hz} operating point (std\,0.61\,ms, 0\% parse failure): naive ZOH must hold each VLA target for roughly 57 control ticks at 100\,Hz.
Nsight NVTX reports \textbf{593.7\,ms} mean ($\approx$1.68\,Hz; $59.4\times$ gap); the $\sim$20\,ms uplift vs.\ wall-clock is consistent with tracing overhead.
Both profilers attribute GPU time to large GEMMs; Nsight additionally highlights H2D pressure (\texttt{cudaMemcpyAsync}), motivating pinned transfers and keeping the ESN on-device between sparse VLA updates.

\mbox{Table~\ref{tab:esn_sweep}} shows that ridge strength dominates tracking on wipe ep.~0: $\lambda{=}1$ outperforms weaker regularization, and $(\alpha,\lambda)=(0.3,1)$ reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} RMSE with jerk nearly matched to the demonstration (ratio 0.981).
Under-regularized settings inflate RMSE by up to $\sim$60$\times$, so a short ridge sweep is necessary.
Training remains cheap ($\sim$2.9\,kHz on V100), enabling $N$/$\rho$ ablations before closed-loop trials.

The dual-process loop isolates control timing under mock VLA: mean step \textbf{4.03\,ms} (248\,Hz) and steady-state max 6.82\,ms clear the 10\,ms budget, confirming GIL-free multiprocessing.
The MuJoCo wipe oracle drives the trained ESN for 6000 kinematic steps: joint RMSE \textbf{$1.37\times10^{-3}$\,rad}, successful grasp (1047 attached frames), and a 3.46\,m cloth path.
Right-EE RMSE ($\sim$9.6\,cm) and table-contact ratio (0.38\%) show that geometric wipe quality remains open even when joint tracking is tight.
These oracle/mock results are \emph{not} live UnifoLM closed-loop evidence; method comparisons still require the planned multi-trial study.
Orientation IK currently tracks EE position only; full rot6d tracking is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a VLA$+$ESN architecture targeting the \textbf{$\sim$57--59$\times$} gap between measured UnifoLM-VLA-Base inference (573\,ms / 1.745\,Hz PyTorch; 594\,ms / 1.684\,Hz Nsight) and G1 100\,Hz control.
A CUDA ridge ESN reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} offline and \textbf{$1.37\times10^{-3}$\,rad} under MuJoCo wipe-oracle replay with successful cloth grasp.
A GIL-free dual-process loop sustains \textbf{248\,Hz} mean control under mock VLA, clearing the 100\,Hz budget ahead of live UnifoLM trials and S2R/DIRT transfer toward ICRA 2027.
