\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models provide open-vocabulary manipulation policies, but their inference rates are incompatible with the 100\,Hz joint control loops required by humanoid robots such as the Unitree G1 (29\,DoF).
We propose a hybrid controller in which a fixed Echo State Network (ESN) reservoir bridges sparse VLA joint targets to smooth 100\,Hz commands.
The reservoir is untrained; only a linear readout is fit by ridge regression on demonstration trajectories.
On a DGX Station (Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB), live \texttt{UnifoLM-VLA-Base} profiling on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table} yields a consistent $\sim$57--59$\times$ gap to 100\,Hz: PyTorch wall-clock latency is \textbf{573.2\,ms} mean (\textbf{1.745\,Hz}; $n{=}100$; P95\,574.1\,ms), while NVIDIA Nsight Systems NVTX ranges on the same action path average \textbf{593.7\,ms} (\textbf{1.684\,Hz}; 3 profiled steps).
Operator-level traces show GEMM-dominated compute (\texttt{magma\_sgemmEx} / \texttt{aten::linear}) and heavy H2D traffic under Nsight (\texttt{cudaMemcpyAsync} $\approx$63.5\% of CUDA API time).
A CUDA ESN ridge readout trained on \texttt{G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} (episode~0; $N{=}1000$, $\rho{=}0.95$) reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} joint RMSE at leak rate $\alpha{=}0.3$ and ridge $\lambda{=}1.0$ (best of a $3{\times}3$ leak$\times$ridge sweep), with training throughput $\sim$2.9\,kHz on V100.
A GIL-free dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN loop (mock VLA @ 2\,Hz, 10\,s wipe-table) sustains \textbf{248\,Hz} mean control (mean step 4.03\,ms; steady-state max 6.82\,ms $<$ 10\,ms), verifying that the reservoir bridge clears the 100\,Hz budget once VLA inference is off the control process.
We evaluate VLA$+$ESN against zero-order hold and linear-interpolation baselines on a MuJoCo G1 wipe-table task, and release an S2R deploy stack for sim-to-real transfer under a DIRT-style discrepancy parameterization.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Foundation VLAs such as OpenVLA~\cite{OpenVLA2025}, $\pi_0$~\cite{Preprints2026}, and Unitree UnifoLM-VLA~\cite{UnifoLM2026} map language and vision to robot actions.
Humanoid whole-body control, however, demands millisecond-scale updates~\cite{PhysicalAI2026}.
Autoregressive or diffusion action heads introduce latency that, under naive hold, yields jerky motion and task failure---the \emph{frequency gap}.

We treat the G1 as a unified dynamical system and insert a leaky ESN between VLA intents ($\sim$1.7\,Hz measured) and joint commands (100\,Hz).
The VLA supplies sparse targets; the reservoir supplies reflexive temporal filtering without backpropagation through time.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(i)~Dual-instrument UnifoLM-VLA-Base latency on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table}: 100-trial PyTorch profiler + Nsight Systems NVTX/CUDA timeline;
(ii)~CUDA ESN with $u(t)=[q_t;\,q^{\star}_{\mathrm{VLA}}]\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$ and ridge readout, with a measured leak$\times$ridge sweep on wipe-table demonstrations;
(iii)~GIL-free dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN control with measured $>100$\,Hz headroom under mock VLA;
(iv)~MuJoCo wipe evaluation and ZOH/linear baselines;
(v)~S2R ZMQ deploy package (29-DoF defaults) for DIRT-guided transfer.

\section{Related Work}
VLA surveys highlight bimanual and whole-body settings as hard cases~\cite{VLA_Review2026}.
Flow matching accelerates action generation~\cite{Preprints2026}; recurrent bridges such as xLSTM help long-horizon memory~\cite{Notes2Self2026}.
Diffusion action chunking trades compute for smoothness; Central Pattern Generators (CPGs) provide rhythmic priors but lack language grounding.
Reservoir computing offers fast, training-light dynamics~\cite{Jaeger2001,Caoetal2023}.
We combine a frozen reservoir with a language-conditioned VLA rather than replacing either.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Perception / VLA:} \texttt{unitreerobotics/UnifoLM-VLA-Base} with VLM backbone \texttt{UnifoLM-VLM-Base} emits 29-D joint actions for clean/wipe-table language goals (e.g., \texttt{g1\_clean\_table}); EE-space variants map poses to joints via Jacobian IK when needed.
  \item \textbf{ESN bridge:} Leaky update
  \[
  x(t)=(1-\alpha)x(t-1)+\alpha\,\tanh\!\big(W_{\mathrm{res}}x(t-1)+W_{\mathrm{in}}u(t)\big),
  \]
  with sparse $W_{\mathrm{res}}$ ($N{=}1000$, spectral radius $\rho{=}0.95$, sparsity $0.9$) and readout
  $y(t)=W_{\mathrm{out}}[x(t);u(t)]$ fit by ridge regression on 100\,Hz joint trajectories with VLA tokens held for 50 ticks ($u\in\mathbb{R}^{58}$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^{29}$).
  We sweep leak rate $\alpha\in\{0.05,0.1,0.3\}$ and ridge $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2},1\}$ (9 configs) and select by RMSE$\times$jerk score.
  \item \textbf{S2R / DIRT layer:} Parameterizes sim--real discrepancy for controller and domain ablations (ESN vs.\ passthrough ZOH/raw) in the deploy stack.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baselines}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pure VLA ZOH:} hold each $\sim$1.7\,Hz target for $\sim$57--59 control ticks at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ linear interpolation:} interpolate between sparse knots at 100\,Hz.
  \item \textbf{VLA $+$ ESN (ours):} CUDA reservoir + ridge readout.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\subsection{Setup}
\textbf{Robot:} Unitree G1, 29 actuated joints.
\textbf{Profiling task:} \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table} with instruction \emph{``Clean the table by moving all clutter items into the bin.''} (UnifoLM-VLA-Base, \texttt{unnorm\_key=g1\_clean\_table}, FP16, \texttt{max\_new\_tokens=64}).
\textbf{Offline / closed-loop task:} Wipe-table (dataset \texttt{G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table}~\cite{UnifoLM2026}).
\textbf{Hardware:} NVIDIA DGX Station, Tesla V100-DGXS-32GB (CUDA).
\textbf{Control:} 100\,Hz MuJoCo kinematic/PD replay and dual-process VLA$\leftrightarrow$ESN integration.
\textbf{Profilers:}
(1)~PyTorch \texttt{torch.profiler} on action generation (3 profile steps) plus a 100-trial wall-clock latency benchmark (run \texttt{pytorch\_profiler\_20260730\_152406});
(2)~Nsight Systems \texttt{nsys profile --trace=cuda,nvtx,osrt} with NVTX ranges \texttt{vla\_action\_generation\_\{warmup,profiled\}} (1 warmup + 3 profiled steps; run \texttt{nsight\_systems\_20260730\_153855}).
\textbf{ESN training:} CUDA ridge on \texttt{unitreerobotics/G1\_Dex1\_Wipe\_Table} episode~0 (1181 train steps after washout 50; control 100\,Hz / VLA hold 2\,Hz).
Sweep artifacts: \texttt{results/step2\_training/esn\_hyperparam\_sweep.csv}; selected checkpoint \texttt{models/esn\_cuda\_ridge/}.
\textbf{Dual-process:} Multiprocessing VLA process (mock, 2\,Hz target) $\leftrightarrow$ MuJoCo$+$CUDA ESN control process (100\,Hz target), 10\,s wipe-table, G1 29-DoF MJCF; report \texttt{results/step3\_dual\_thread/dual\_thread\_report.json}.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Table~\ref{tab:measured} cross-validates the frequency gap with two instruments.
The PyTorch 100-trial campaign establishes a stable \textbf{573\,ms / 1.745\,Hz} operating point (std\,0.61\,ms, 0\% parse failure): naive ZOH must hold each VLA target for roughly 57 control ticks at 100\,Hz.
Nsight Systems NVTX on the same \texttt{infer()} path reports \textbf{593.7\,ms} mean over three profiled steps ($\approx$1.68\,Hz; 59.4$\times$ gap); the $\sim$20\,ms uplift vs.\ the wall-clock campaign is consistent with tracing overhead rather than a second failure mode.
Both profilers attribute GPU time primarily to large GEMMs (\texttt{magma\_sgemmEx} / \texttt{aten::linear}/\texttt{mm}/\texttt{addmm}); Nsight additionally highlights H2D pressure (\texttt{cudaMemcpyAsync} dominates CUDA API time), motivating pinned/non-blocking transfers and keeping the ESN on-device between sparse VLA updates.

Table~\ref{tab:esn_sweep} shows that ridge strength dominates tracking fidelity on wipe-table ep.~0: $\lambda{=}1$ consistently outperforms $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-2}\}$, and the selected $(\alpha,\lambda)=(0.3,1)$ checkpoint reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} RMSE with jerk nearly matched to the demonstration (ratio 0.981).
Under-regularized / poorly leaked settings inflate RMSE by up to $\sim$60$\times$ (worst $5.85\times10^{-2}$\,rad), underscoring that the ESN bridge is not ``plug-and-play'' without a short ridge sweep.
Training remains cheap ($\sim$2.9\,kHz ridge/solve steps on V100 for 1181 washout-trimmed samples), so ablations over $N$ and $\rho$ remain feasible before closed-loop trials.

The dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN experiment (Table~\ref{tab:measured}, bottom) isolates control-loop timing with a mock VLA: mean step latency \textbf{4.03\,ms} (248\,Hz), P99 5.42\,ms, and steady-state max 6.82\,ms---well under the 10\,ms / 100\,Hz budget---confirming that multiprocessing removes the Python GIL from the critical path.
A 45.5\,ms startup spike does not persist after warm-up; 20 VLA ticks over 10\,s match the 2\,Hz perception target.
This establishes timing headroom for the ESN bridge; paper-facing wipe success rates still require live UnifoLM closed-loop runs, and we do not treat mock Bernoulli priors as task-success evidence.
Orientation IK currently tracks EE position only; full rot6d tracking is future work.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a VLA$+$ESN architecture targeting the \textbf{$\sim$57--59$\times$} frequency gap between measured UnifoLM-VLA-Base inference (PyTorch 573\,ms / 1.745\,Hz; Nsight NVTX 594\,ms / 1.684\,Hz on \texttt{G1\_Clean\_Table}) and G1 100\,Hz control.
A CUDA ridge ESN on wipe-table demonstrations reaches \textbf{$9.83\times10^{-4}$\,rad} RMSE at $(\alpha,\lambda)=(0.3,1)$ after a measured $3{\times}3$ hyperparameter sweep.
A GIL-free dual-process MuJoCo$+$ESN loop sustains \textbf{248\,Hz} mean control (steady max 6.82\,ms) under mock VLA, clearing the 100\,Hz budget before live UnifoLM closed-loop trials and S2R/DIRT transfer toward ICRA 2027.
